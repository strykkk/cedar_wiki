GABRIELLE PERSONAL HISTORY
Gabrielle was born into a southern cotton-farming family with six siblings. In her memory, the times described by her elders grew increasingly bleak: water shortages, oil crises, falling cotton prices, black riots...all those new things being hyped up out there, Nox development, computer technology, the internet, financial innovation... none of it had anything to do with them.
Despite breaking with tradition, Gabrielle chose the path of enlisting in the military. Her impoverished upbringing had left her afflicted with severe hoarding tendencies: she felt compelled to carry everything she saw, convinced it might prove useful someday. Her large rucksack was crammed with all manner of odds and ends, some discarded by other members of the mission. Jacqueline naturally disliked her soldiers carrying excessive extra weight, but Gabrielle's thriftiness seemed to have become a kind of dogma. Her mantra was "It would be God's punishment...", the very thought of discarding anything felt like a sin.
Despite her best efforts to conceal it, her colleagues soon realised Gabrielle was from Alabama, her thick accent giving her away. When the conversation turned to Southern cuisine in search of a topic, they discovered Gabrielle had never actually tasted these dishes. Her notion of Southern delicacies mirrored the stereotypical image of African-American food: fried chicken and watermelon, that sort of thing. Thus, within the mission, Gabrielle alone genuinely regarded Manpreti's curry concoction as a delicacy, she had never before encountered such richly flavoured cuisine. Though Manpreti often shared food with her, he never offered the truly exquisite dishes, after all, a merchant's fundamental skill lies in managing customers' expectations.
Manpreti soon discovered that this insecure soldier proved an exceptionally cheap labourer, requiring only the most trivial material incentives to secure her enthusiastic service. However, Manpreti was not the only one who, consciously or unconsciously, treated Gabrielle as a tool. Jacqueline was consumed by a fierce competitive spirit, Bella was particularly keen to validate her own combat theories and even Helen would unconsciously put her to work, whether it was Jacqueline's charisma, Manpreti's material rewards, Helen's warmth, or Bella's elaborate theories...all seemed to make it difficult for her to refuse.



GABRIELLE PERSONAL REPORT
Gabrielle, a hardworking and resilient sharpshooter in the Vespuccian Marine Corps,She is prone to subservience due to her low self-esteem. While obedience is a commendable quality for a soldier, her inferiority complex often hinders her performance on the battlefield. Her superiors and comrades try to encourage her, but their words are difficult for the less educated Gabrielle to understand, and their enthusiasm only makes her feel more uneasy.
Gabrielle was born into a cotton-farming family in the South with six siblings. In her memory, the situation described by her parents always seemed to get worse: water shortages, oil crises, falling cotton prices, black riots... All those new things that were being hyped up outside, Nox development, computer technology, the internet, financial innovation had absolutely nothing to do with them.
Gabrielle's impoverished upbringing caused her to develop severe hamsterism: she wanted to take everything she saw, always feeling it might come in handy. Her large backpack was overflowing with all sorts of junk, some of which had been discarded by other members of the mission. Jacqueline certainly didn't want her soldiers to carry too much extra weight, but Gabrielle's frugality seemed to have become a kind of dogma, as her catchphrase was "God will punish me..." and the thought of possibly throwing something away felt like a sin.
Manprit, a supplier for the Ruvilian Mission, quickly discovered that this self-conscious soldier was exceptionally cheap labor; a small material reward was all it took to secure her enthusiastic service. However, Manprit wasn't the only one intentionally or unintentionally using Gabrielle as a tool. The mission commanders were either driven by competitiveness or eager to test their own operational theories… This ordinary Marine's journey to Nox was destined to be fraught with difficulties.
